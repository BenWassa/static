# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Self-discovering autistic and AuDHD adults, typically aged 20–45, reading during or after
a period of questioning. Often arriving depleted: cognitively overloaded, in burnout, or
processing a recent realisation. Reading on a phone, likely at home or in transit, not at
a desk. High intelligence, low tolerance for reassurance theater. Monotropic attention —
they will follow a thread deep if it has their interest, then need to escape cleanly.
Working memory is real and limited. Sensory sensitivity applies to interfaces too.

The job: understand the neurological reality behind their specific experiences, and find
language and strategies they can actually use. Not diagnosis. Not scores. Recognition.

## Product Purpose

Static is a calm, scannable, mobile-first reading experience that turns neuroscience
research into mechanical explanation. It exists because most autistic adults encounter
their own nervous system as mystery, pathology, or moral failure — Static treats it as
engineering: here is the mechanism, here is what helps, here is why.

Success looks like a reader finishing a behaviour card and thinking "that is exactly what
happens, and now I understand why." Not "I feel validated." Understanding, not comfort.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, rigorous, dignified.

Academic confidence without the hierarchy. Cool intelligence over performed warmth. The
voice of a knowledgeable peer who assumes you can handle the full picture. No hedging, no
softening, no "it can sometimes feel like..." — just the mechanism, stated plainly.

The interface should feel like a well-made book, not an app. A quiet library in which
nothing competes for attention except the content.

## Anti-references

- **Wellness apps** (Headspace, Calm, any pastel + round-corner UI): infantilising,
  decorative calm, treats complexity as a problem. The opposite register.
- **NeuroSpicy / ADHD influencer content**: bold neon, energetic microtype, memes. Loud
  where Static should be still.
- **Medical / clinical portals**: utilitarian grids, healthcare-teal palettes, "patient
  portal" hierarchy. Cold and authoritative in the wrong direction.
- **SaaS product landing pages**: hero metrics, gradient text, benefit bullets, CTA
  towers. The wrong category reflex entirely.
- **Notion public pages**: beige + Helvetica + database tables. The knowledge-tool
  aesthetic bleeds its context into everything. Static is a reading experience, not a
  workspace.

## Design Principles

1. **The interface practices what the content preaches.** Static describes how autistic
   nervous systems work. The interface itself must demonstrate it understands them: low
   sensory load, predictable structure, clean escape paths, no surprise.

2. **Rigour is the warmth.** Precision, mechanical language, and taking the reader
   seriously IS the care. No softening. Vagueness is condescending; accuracy is respect.

3. **Depth on demand, surface always readable.** The full site is comprehensible at the
   surface layer; all detail is one tap deeper. The reader controls density at every point.

4. **Nothing competes.** No decorative motion, no ambient sound, no elements that draw
   attention they don't deserve. Every visual element serves orientation or content.
   If it doesn't earn its presence, it isn't there.

5. **Works for someone in burnout.** The actual use case is a person in cognitive overload
   on a phone with one bar of signal. Fast, private, robust, keyboard navigable,
   screen-reader friendly, no tracking. The test: could someone who is overwhelmed find
   what they need without expending effort on the interface?

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA as the floor.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: all animation removed or reduced to a single frame. This is
  a non-negotiable given the audience.
- Color contrast: meet AA everywhere; push toward AAA for body text given the reading
  depth and depleted-state use case.
- Screen-reader and keyboard navigation throughout. No interaction model that requires
  mouse precision or hover state.
- No scores, no assessments, no severity labels. The tools (Capacity Cup, Sensory Profile)
  visualise — they do not judge.
